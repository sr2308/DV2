From ocean to plate

In 2019-20, seafood farms in Australia earned more than wild-catch fishing for the first time. This page looks at how the industry got there, where our seafood comes from and how much of what we eat is imported.

Chapter 1

What does Australia produce?

Which seafood is the industry built on? Australia produces hundreds of seafood species, but only a few of them make up most of the value. In Figure 1, each box is sized by value and coloured by whether it was farmed or wild-caught.

$1.3B

Farmed salmon and trout, almost all from Tasmania, were worth $1.3 billion in 2023-24. That is more than rock lobster, prawns and oysters added together.

Rock lobster ($489M) is still the most valuable wild catch, followed by wild prawns ($225M). The rest is split between tuna, abalone, pearls and hundreds of fish species.

Five products (salmon, rock lobster, prawns, oysters and tuna) make up about two-thirds of the industry's value.

Where does it come from?

Which parts of Australia produce the most seafood? Most of the value comes from a few states and a few coastal areas, and each state tends to focus on different seafood. Click a state in Figure 2 to highlight it in Figure 4, and click it again to reset.

Tasmania's salmon farms and Western Australia's lobster fishery make up a large part of the industry.

Chapter 3

Farmed or caught?

Is farming replacing fishing? Most of Australia's seafood used to be caught in the wild. In 2019-20 farmed seafood was worth more than wild-caught for the first time, and the Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics (ABARES) expects the gap to keep growing.

Australia now earns more from farming seafood than from catching it, and ABARES expects the gap to get bigger.

Chapter 5

An industry in transition

What has changed, and what ends up on our plates? In 1998-99, rock lobster was Australia's most valuable seafood and salmon was sixth. Salmon is now first. At the same time, most of the seafood Australians eat is imported, and some wild fish stocks are depleted.

Farming has overtaken fishing in Australia, but most of the seafood we eat still comes from overseas.

  • Farming now earns more than fishing, and the gap is expected to grow.
  • Rock lobster, the most valuable wild catch, depends mostly on one export market.
  • About six in every ten kilograms of seafood eaten here is imported.
  • Most assessed wild stocks are sustainable, but some are depleted, especially in Tasmania.
